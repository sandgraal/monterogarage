## Signing in

Your garage belongs to an account, and getting one takes no password.

1. Go to **Sign in**.
2. Either **type your email** and we send you a one-time sign-in link, **or**
   choose **Continue with Google**.
3. Open the link from the same browser. That is it — you are in.

There is **no password to choose or forget**. The email link works once, and
only from the device's browser session that requested it. Everything you store
is **private from the very start**; nothing is published until you publish it.

> Signing in and using your garage need JavaScript, because your data is fetched
> into your own browser after you sign in. The reference side of the site works
> without an account and without JavaScript.

## Creating your vehicle profile

From your garage, choose **Add a vehicle**. A profile has four parts:

**1. What you call it.** A name only you see (until you publish something) — for
example, the truck this site was built around is called _Gitana Blanca_.

**2. Which truck it is.** This is what lets parts, procedures, and problems match
your truck. **Only the generation is required.** Market, year, and engine are
optional — if you are not sure, choose **"Not sure yet"** and leave them
unanswered. You can always fill them in later. The lists only ever offer
combinations that actually existed, so you cannot build an impossible truck.

**3. Odometer.** Enter the reading as it stands today, and pick your unit —
**kilometres or miles**. The number is stored once and always shown back in the
unit you chose.

**4. Photos** (see below).

You can **edit** or **delete** a vehicle at any time. Deleting a vehicle also
removes its photos and everything recorded on it, and that cannot be undone — so
the site asks you to confirm first.

## Photos and the cover photo

Add photos to any vehicle with **Add a photo**. Accepted image types are JPEG,
PNG, WebP, AVIF, and HEIC.

Photos live in **private storage**: nobody without your session can open one, and
the temporary links the page uses to show them expire on their own.

You can mark one photo as the **cover** with **Set as cover**. The cover is the
photo that represents the truck — and it is the image shown on your public
**showcase page** if you choose to publish one.

## Records and receipts

A **record** is one entry in your truck's history. Add one with **Add a record**.
Every record has:

- **Date** — when it happened.
- **Kind of record** — one of:
  - **Work done** — something you (or a shop) did to the truck.
  - **Receipt** — a purchase.
  - **Note** — anything worth remembering.
  - **Planned** — something you intend to do (this feeds the _Planned work_ view).
- **What it was** — a one-line title you will recognise later, e.g. _"Front
  sway-bar end links replaced."_
- **Notes** — as much detail as you like, in whichever language you think in.
- **Cost** and **currency** — optional. Amounts are added up per currency and
  never converted between them.
- **Time it took** — optional, in hours or minutes.
- **Odometer that day** — optional; it does not change the truck's current
  reading.
- **Reference links** — connect the record to the **problems**, **parts**, and
  **procedures** pages the job used. Linking them is how your record and the
  reference find each other later (and how your record can appear as
  [community evidence](#community-evidence) if you choose).

### Attaching receipts

Open a record and use **Attach receipt**. A receipt can carry the **vendor**
(where it is from), the **date on the receipt**, and the **amount**. Accepted
files are JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, and PDF.

### Attaching photos, video, and voice notes

A record can also hold **photos, video, and voice notes** that are not receipts —
a photo of the part that failed, a video of a noise, the voice note the shop sent
you. Use **Attach file** in the _Photos, video and voice notes_ section.

Receipts and attachments live in the same **private storage** as your photos.
Nobody without your session can open them.

## Hiding costs

Costs are treated as separate from everything else, on purpose. You can record
what a job cost and still keep that number to yourself, even while sharing the
rest.

This shows up in two places:

- **On a public work-log** (see below), each record has **two independent
  switches**: _Show this record_ and _Show what it cost_. A record you have made
  public still keeps its cost — and its receipts — private until you open those
  too.
- **On a private share link**, _Show what each job cost_ and _Show the receipts_
  are **two separate choices**. You can hand over the scans without the totals,
  or the totals without the scans.

When a cost is genuinely not recorded (a warranty job, a favour, a parts swap in
your own driveway), the site says _"not recorded"_ rather than pretending it was
free.

## The three garage views

Open a vehicle and you get three tabs. All three are **worked out from your
records** — the site never invents anything.

- **Timeline** — everything you have recorded, in order.
- **Current state** — the latest odometer reading in your records, a **service
  history** (one line per reference entry you have linked, longest-untouched
  first), and **open items**.
- **Planned work** — your planned records in date order (past-due first), with a
  rough total of the **time and cost you estimated**.

Two things this deliberately does **not** do:

- It **never tells you a service is "due."** A due date needs a service interval
  the records cannot know. The sheet reports elapsed time and distance; _"past
  its date"_ only means a date **you wrote** has passed.
- It **sends no reminders.**

If a view cannot load your records, it says so plainly rather than showing an
empty sheet — because an empty sheet would be a false statement about your truck.

## Browsing the reference as your truck

Once a vehicle's market, year, and engine are filled in, you can choose **Browse
the site as this truck**. The whole reference library then filters to your exact
truck — parts, procedures, and problems that fit _your_ generation, market, year,
and engine. You can clear the filter at any time.

## Making a page public

Publishing is entirely optional and always under your control. It happens in two
steps.

### Step 1 — Choose your public address

Pick the name your published pages live under (the site calls it your
**address** — it is the middle of your public URL). It is between 3 and a few
dozen characters, using lower-case letters, numbers, and hyphens.

Your address is **yours alone, and stays yours even if you change it later** — a
link you have already given someone never later leads to a stranger's garage.

### Step 2 — Choose what is public

Nothing is public until you say so, **one page at a time and one record at a
time**. You have two public pages you can switch on independently:

- **The showcase page** — the truck's photos and its profile (the cover photo
  leads).
- **The work-log page** — only the records you open, one by one. **Never your
  whole history at once.**

On the work-log, each record has the two switches described under
[Hiding costs](#hiding-costs): _Show this record_ and, separately, _Show what it
cost_. **Receipts are never shown on a public work-log at all.**

### What always stays private

Even with both pages public:

- Records you have **not** opened stay private.
- Costs stay private unless you open them per record.
- **Receipts and file attachments are never public.**
- Your vehicle's edit controls, your other vehicles, and your account are yours
  alone.

Anyone visiting a published page is shown a note that the records are **the
owner's own account of their own truck** — never presented as verified facts.

## Sharing with one specific person

Publishing puts a page on the open web. **Sharing** does the opposite: it hands
**one person** a private link to a truck's history, and no one else.

Use **Share this truck with one person**. You decide:

- **Who it is for** — _A mechanic_ or _A buyer_. These labels just say who the
  link is for; what it actually opens is decided by the switches below.
- **Show what each job cost** — off by default.
- **Show the receipts** — a separate switch from costs.
- **Ends after** — pick an expiry in days. The link ends on its own.
- **Send to a specific person (optional)** — enter the email of _their_ account
  to tie the link to it once they sign in; then it opens only for them. Leave it
  blank for a link anyone you send it to can open.

When you create the link, it is **shown once and cannot be shown again.** Copy it
right away. Anyone who holds the link can open it, so **send it the way you would
send a key.** If you lose it, end that one and create another.

### Managing your links

Every link you have made is listed, showing what it opens and when it ends. From
the list you can:

- **End this link** (revoke it) immediately — this is never gated or delayed.
- **Extend until revoked** — turn a link with an expiry date into one that lasts
  until you end it. (Most useful for a mechanic you work with regularly.)

A person you sent a link to who signs in can add the truck to their **roster**, so
they never have to find the link again — see [[For Mechanics|For-Mechanics]].

> There is also a switch, **"Also let their shop see it,"** off by default. It is
> a consent control for a shop feature that is still on the way — see
> [[Roadmap|Roadmap]]. Sharing with a person is not sharing with their business.

### A shared link vs. a published page

|                    | Shared link              | Published page          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Who can see it     | Only who you send it to  | Anyone on the web       |
| Costs              | Your choice, per link    | Your choice, per record |
| Receipts           | Your choice, per link    | Never                   |
| Expires            | Yes (or "until revoked") | No — you unpublish it   |
| On the public site | No                       | Yes                     |

## Community evidence

If you keep a record that documents a known problem, you can choose to link that
record to the matching **problem page** in the reference. When you do — and only
if you have published that record — it can appear on the problem page under
**"First-hand evidence from owners."**

This is how the reference gets grounded in real trucks. Your record is always
shown as **your own account of your own truck**, kept exactly as you wrote it,
with a link back to your published work-log. The site never turns it into a
verified fact.

This is entirely opt-in: a record only appears there if you have both **published
it** and **linked it to the problem.**
